# How Memory Works

Memory is the ability to retain and reproduce information. It involves three processes.
- **Encoding** is the conversion of information into a form in which it can be stored.
- **Storage** is the retention of encoded information over time.
- **Retrieval** is access to stored information when it is needed.

## The Atkinson–Shiffrin model
Information passes through three stores in order.
- **Sensory memory** is a store that holds impressions from the senses for a fraction of a second while the brain decides what to pay attention to.
- **Short-term memory** is a store that holds a small amount of information (about 4–7 items) for a few seconds while it is being worked with.
- **Long-term memory** is a store that holds information indefinitely and with almost no limit on capacity.
What receives attention enters short-term memory. What is rehearsed and linked to what is already known enters long-term memory.

## The forgetting curve
The **forgetting curve** is a graph that shows how the share of retained information falls over time without review. It was described by Hermann Ebbinghaus.
- Forgetting is strongest in the first hours and days after learning.
- After that, forgetting slows down.
- Each review makes the curve flatter: the next round of forgetting is slower.
